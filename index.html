How the SIP calculator works

A SIP invests a fixed amount every month. Each instalment earns the expected monthly return, and the gains earn returns of their own. That compounding is why starting early matters more than investing big.

FV = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)
P = monthly amount, i = annual rate ÷ 12, n = months

For lumpsum, the formula is FV = P × (1 + r)ᵗ, where r is the annual return and t is the number of years. With step-up, the monthly amount rises by your chosen percentage at the start of every year.

Frequently asked questions

What is a SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan lets you invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month. It builds discipline and averages your purchase cost over market ups and downs.

SIP or lumpsum: which is better?

SIP suits regular income and lowers timing risk. Lumpsum suits idle surplus cash and can do better when markets rise. Many investors use both.

What is a step-up SIP?

A step-up SIP increases your instalment by a fixed percentage each year, so your investing keeps pace with your salary.

What return should I enter?

Long-term equity funds have historically returned roughly 10–14% a year in India, but this varies and is never guaranteed. Try a few values to see a range.

Are these results guaranteed?

No. Mutual fund returns are market-linked. The calculator shows an estimate based only on what you enter, before taxes and expense ratios.
